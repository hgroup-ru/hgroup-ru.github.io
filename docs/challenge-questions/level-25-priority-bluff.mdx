<TabItem value="question">

Alice deliberately plays a fully-known blue 2 instead of a higher-Priority known card. Bob has no clued blue 3 and cannot see a blue 3 in anyone else's hand. His own hand is completely unclued.

What should Bob do, and what does he learn if the blind-play is not blue 3?

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

Bob should blind-play his **Finesse Position**. With no Prompt and no visible blue 3 elsewhere, the Priority signal can be a **Priority Bluff** on Bob. If his blind-play is not blue 3, he learns that he was Bluffed and that no one has the blue 3 connection Alice appeared to promise.
