<TabItem value="question">

Red 2 is played. Alice gives Bob a number 4 Play Clue touching one card. Bob sees no playable cards in Alice's or Cathy's hand. The red 4 is the closest 4 to being playable, and Bob sees no red 3 in either other hand.

Should Bob blind-play his own Finesse Position?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

**Yes.** The clue points Bob toward red 4, and there is no visible red 3 that can connect to it. Bob therefore concludes that the missing red 3 must be in his own Finesse Position and blind-plays it. It turns out to be the blue 1 and plays successfully: the immediate mismatch reveals a legal **Self-Bluff**. At level 11 this works with a rank clue; a color Self-Bluff is not part of the level-11 convention.

The useful final notes are the same ones shown by the official Self-Bluff example: Bob marks his first card as the Bluff reaction and records **r4** on the clued 4.
