<TabItem value="question">

All 2s were already played. Alice began with one red-clued card that, from her information, could be `r3`, `r4`, or `r5`.

Alice gave Cathy a red Play Clue on `r4`. Cathy waited. Bob then played his blind Finesse Position as `r3` instead of Certain Discarding it. Later, Cathy played her `r4` instead of Sarcastic Discarding it. The turn has now returned to Alice; the red stack is on 4.

What can Alice conclude about her own hidden red card, and what should she do now?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

Alice can conclude that her red card is exactly `r5`, so she should play it.

- Bob's choice to **play** the blind `r3` eliminates `r3` from Alice's possibilities: if Alice actually had the duplicated `r3`, Bob would have been able to Certain Discard.
- Cathy's later choice to **play** `r4` eliminates `r4`: if Alice actually had the duplicated `r4`, Cathy would Sarcastic Discard it back.
- The only remaining possibility is `r5`. Since red is now stacked through 4, Alice should play that card.

The original move combined the Sarcastic Finesse and Certain Finesse mechanisms; this is a Composition Finesse.

</TabItem>
