<TabItem value="question">

No 1s are played. Bob has just discarded a **known exact `r1`**, creating one clue. Alice has one rank-1-clued card, but she does not know its color. Cathy visibly has `r1` in Finesse Position and `y5` on chop, with no known immediate action.

It is Alice's turn. Should she play her own known-playable 1, trusting Cathy to play the `r1`, or should she spend the clue to 5 Save Cathy's `y5`?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

Alice should 5 Save Cathy's `y5` now.

Alice must preserve two live possibilities for her own hidden 1:

- If Alice's 1 is itself `r1`, then the three physical copies are Bob's discarded `r1`, Alice's hidden `r1`, and Cathy's visible `r1`. Bob's discard is interpreted as a Sarcastic Discard to Alice, which takes precedence over a Gentleman's Discard; Cathy has no transferred action.
- If Alice's 1 is another color, Bob's discarded `r1` can be a Gentleman's Discard to Cathy's Finesse Position, so Cathy will play.

The priority rule does not tell Alice which hidden world she is in. She also cannot rely on Cathy giving a clue into Alice's own unknown hand. Saving `y5` is the action that is safe in both possibilities.
