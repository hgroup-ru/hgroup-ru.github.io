<TabItem value="question">

Red 1 is played. Cathy's hand from newest to oldest is red 4, yellow 2, blue 4, blue 5, red 2. Alice clues number 5 to slot 4, Chop Moving Cathy's playable red 2 on chop. A direct red clue would also touch red 4, and a direct number 2 clue would also touch yellow 2; neither extra touch is useful now.

Should Bob treat the strange Chop Move of a playable card as a Chop Move Ignition and blind-play?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

**No.** A Chop Move Ignition exists only when there is no other good reason to Chop Move the playable card. Here the Chop Move avoids a **Good Touch Principle** problem: both obvious direct Play Clues would unnecessarily touch another unplayable card. Because the Chop Move already has independent value, Bob must not manufacture an extra Ignition from it.
