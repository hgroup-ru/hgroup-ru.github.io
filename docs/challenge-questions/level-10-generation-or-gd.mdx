<TabItem value="question">

The red stack is on 3 and there are 0 clues. Alice has two clued red cards that she has fully identified as `r4` and `r5`. Bob's Finesse Position is the other `r4`; his `b2` is on chop and his old `b5` is already clued. Cathy's `y5` is on chop.

If Alice simply plays her `r4`, Bob will have to discard `b2` to generate a clue for Cathy's `y5`. Alice could instead discard an unknown chop card herself as a Generation Discard.

Is there a better Level 10 line?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

Yes. Alice should discard her known `r4` as a Gentleman's Discard.

1. Alice discards `r4`, creating one clue.
2. Bob plays the transferred `r4`; red advances to 4.
3. Cathy gives Bob a blue Play Clue: the previously untouched `b2` is the focus, while the old `b5` is filled in.
4. Alice's known `r5` is now playable. She plays it and regains a clue token from playing a 5.
5. Bob uses that clue to 5 Save Cathy's `y5` before her turn.

Compared with the Generation Discard, this line avoids risking an unknown chop card, advances the red stack, preserves `b2`, and still produces the clue needed to save `y5`.

</TabItem>
