<TabItem value="question">

Alice clues one or more newly touched cards that everyone knows are trash. The team can rule out a Trash Chop Move, and it can also rule out both Trash Push and Trash Push Finesse. What interpretation remains? How can an observer or the clue-receiver know those ordinary interpretations are impossible, and if a Bad Trash Chop Move Ejection is technically imaginable, which interpretation has priority?

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

The clue communicates a **Trash Double Ignition**. For a Trash Chop Move, a player other than the clue-receiver can rule it out by seeing that the Chop Moved card is bad; the clue-receiver can rule it out when there are no cards left to Chop Move, meaning the remaining cards are accounted for or could receive direct Play or Save Clues. For Trash Push or Trash Push Finesse, an observer can similarly rule it out by seeing that the pushed card is bad, while the clue-receiver can rule it out when there are no cards left to Trash Push. When neither family can explain the clue, TDI is the interpretation. Players should prefer Trash Double Ignition over a **Bad Trash Chop Move Ejection**, which Level 21 explicitly treats as very rare and later material. Trash Double Ignition, unlike Replay and Poke Double Ignition, is restricted to toward the end of the game.

</TabItem>
