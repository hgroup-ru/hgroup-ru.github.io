<TabItem value="question">

Bob takes an action that would make sense if he had privately deduced the exact identity of one of his own cards. Cathy cannot reconstruct such a deduction from the information available to her, but there is also an ordinary conventional explanation for Bob's action that requires no asymmetric knowledge.

Should Cathy assume that Bob has asymmetric information?

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

**No.** At level 12, players should not assume that another player has asymmetric information by default. Asymmetric knowledge is the exception. Cathy should use the ordinary conventional explanation while one remains available; she should infer that Bob has special private knowledge only when there is no other reasonable explanation for his action.

</TabItem>
