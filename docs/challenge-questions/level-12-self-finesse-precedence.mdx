<TabItem value="question">

Red is on 1 and green is on 2. Bob's chop has old negative green information, but it has never been clued red. Alice now gives Bob a number 3 Play Clue touching a newer card and chop. Because chop cannot be green, the ordinary Focus Inversion reading would put the directly playable green 3 on the newer touched card. Bob can also see yellow 2, blue 2, and purple 2 outside his hand, while green 2 is already played; he sees no red 2, and his own Finesse Position is completely unclued.

Should Bob take the new card as the green-3 focus, or blind-play his Finesse Position first?

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

Bob should **blind-play his Finesse Position first**. The Focus Inversion alternative really does focus the newer touched 3, because chop is known not green. But a rank Self-Finesse is also available: with the other connecting 2s accounted for, Bob can be holding red 2 for the red 3 among the touched cards. Level 12 explicitly gives rank Self-Finesses precedence over Focus Inversion, so Bob tries the Finesse first rather than immediately treating the newer 3 as green 3.
