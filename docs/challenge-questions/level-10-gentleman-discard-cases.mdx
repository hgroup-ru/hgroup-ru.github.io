<TabItem value="question">

In all four positions, all 1s are played. Alice has a clued red card that she has fully identified as `r2`, and Bob has the other `r2` in his Finesse Position. It is Alice's turn.

- **A:** 3 clues are available. There is no extra danger or useful layer.
- **B:** 0 clues are available. Bob has `g3` on chop, and Cathy has a known playable `g2`.
- **C:** 4 clues are available. Bob already has several known playable 2s.
- **D:** 2 clues are available. Bob has a playable `g2` in front of the matching `r2`.

For each position, should Alice perform a Gentleman's Discard with her known `r2`? Distinguish "allowed" from "good".

<Question />

</TabItem>
<TabItem value="solution">

- **A — no.** The GD is allowed, but it only delays the same `r2` play and gains nothing in return.
- **B — yes.** The discard creates a clue, Bob plays the transferred `r2` instead of discarding, Cathy can play `g2`, and Alice gets a turn to clue Bob's now-playable `g3` before he reaches chop again.
- **C — no.** The GD is allowed, but it further concentrates useful actions in Bob's already-busy hand and works against Team Distribution Principle.
- **D — yes.** This is a useful Layered Gentleman's Discard: Bob first blind-plays `g2`, keeps the promise of `r2`, and can continue the layer on a later turn.
